The offsets are rules, not typing. Eight Sleep at T minus 10, Whoop at T minus 5, the iPhone at T, and the paired Apple Watch mirrors the iPhone alarm on its own. Every offset is per device and adjustable.

It moves alarms you already have.

Eight Sleep: reads the existing alarm, changes the time, writes it back. Whoop: reads the existing schedule, changes the time, writes it back. Nothing is authored from scratch, because everything not being changed is echoed back exactly as the server sent it.

01

Read

Pull the alarm or the schedule the service is actually holding right now, and note the time it was read.

02

Change the time

One field moves. Your thermal settings, your vibration settings and your wake mode are carried across untouched, including the fields with no known meaning.

03

Write it back, then read it back

The write is followed by a second read. A response code is not a moved alarm, so the app compares the instant it gets back before it says anything at all.

What survives a write

Your settings are yours.

The app authors one value: the time. Everything else in the payload is echoed back exactly as the server sent it, which is why nothing you chose in the Eight Sleep app or the Whoop app quietly changes underneath you.

And the exception, in the same weight

On iPhone it creates an alarm.

It creates one beside whatever the phone already has, because iOS exposes no way to read or change a Clock alarm. Two of the three legs move an alarm. The third makes one.

Both halves of that have to be said together. The app said the universal version once, and the universal version was wrong.

Any device can be the one that gets changed.

Set 05:00 in the Whoop app for a flight and OneAlarm notices before it writes anything. It does not overwrite you, and it does not pretend the conflict is not there.

Three choices: follow the strap, put the old time back, or leave the strap alone. All three are equal weight in the sheet, and the sheet says why.

Nothing on that screen is styled as the recommended answer, because the app has no opinion about which of your two schedules is the real one.

There is nowhere else for your data to be.

Not a policy page. Five facts about where things are and what the app refuses to send.

  • Keychain

    Credentials and tokens live in the iOS Keychain and nowhere else. Never in UserDefaults, never in plaintext, never logged, never committed.

  • No backend

    No backend. No telemetry. No third party analytics. There is no account to make with anyone, because there is nobody to make it with.

  • Refused addresses

    Destructive, firmware and wipe commands are refused before a request leaves the phone. It cannot change your bed temperature or run the pump.

  • Preview, then send

    The resolved values and the literal outbound request are visible before anything goes out, and the screen states plainly that nothing has been sent.

  • The iPhone leg

    Needs no credentials, no network and no accounts. It works the moment it installs, and it is the one that has to ring.

Five things, stated once, permanently

What it cannot do.

These are not footnotes and they are not a banner you can dismiss. An alarm clock that overstates itself is an alarm clock you find out about at 06:00.

  1. Your phone's own alarms still ring.

    OneAlarm cannot see or change a Clock alarm or the Health sleep schedule. If you already have one set, it keeps going off at its own time, next to the one OneAlarm arms.

    The remedy

    Turn the Alarm toggle off inside the Sleep Schedule. The app says this permanently rather than as a banner you can dismiss and forget.

  2. Whoop holds one schedule per account.

    So it cannot express two routines the way Eight Sleep can. The day list of whichever routine covers tonight is what gets written, and that is the whole of what the strap knows.

  3. Whoop's server holding the time is not your wrist buzzing.

    Those are two different claims and only the first one is verified. The strap takes the new time at its next sync with the Whoop app. That is why Whoop's best state is saved, and why it is amber rather than green.

  4. No snooze.

    Apple's sleep alarm has a nine minute snooze. This has none. If that is the feature you rely on, this is the wrong alarm clock.

  5. Not on the App Store, and not heading there.

    It uses Whoop's and Eight Sleep's own internal web services, which is a question about their terms rather than about Apple's technical rules. It is a personal, single account build.

    It uses no private Apple APIs, so a personal build and TestFlight internal testing are both open routes.
