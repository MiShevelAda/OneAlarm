Three relationships

All three are first class. Nobody here is a degraded version of somebody else, and a person moves between them.

Not everyone wakes up the same way.

The first version of this app assumed everybody keeps a routine. That is one population of three, and possibly not the largest. Day by day for a month, then routines, then back again, with nothing to delete and nothing to migrate.

Routine led

A statement they rarely touch.

Weekdays 07:45, with M T W T F lit. Weekend 10:30, with S S lit. Two sentences about how the week goes, written once. What they need is not a better editor. It is a clean way to bend a single date without touching the rule that produced it.

Day by day

An evening question, asked and answered.

What time do I need to get up tomorrow. That is the whole model. For them a weekday and weekend split is noise, so they never meet the word routine anywhere in the app, and the answer is one gesture on the screen already in front of them.

No alarm at all

A chosen setting, not an empty state.

Bed and wake recommendations, with nothing armed. Both services support this on purpose. An app that reads zero alarms as a misconfiguration to be fixed is simply wrong about these people, so this one reads it as an answer.

The rule, and the exception

Both halves have to be said together. The app once said only the first half, and the first half on its own was wrong.

It moves alarms you already have.

Nothing here is a new alarm competing with your old ones, on two of the three legs. On the third it is exactly that, and saying so is the only honest option available.

On the bed and the strap

Read, change the time, write it back.

Eight Sleep: it reads the alarm that is already there, changes the time, and writes it back. Whoop: it reads the existing schedule, changes the time, and writes it back.

Your thermal settings, your vibration settings and your wake mode survive, because they are echoed back exactly as the server sent them, including fields with no known meaning. Nothing you chose is authored by this app.

On the iPhone

It creates one, beside the ones you have.

iOS exposes no way to read or change a Clock alarm or the Health sleep schedule, so on the phone OneAlarm cannot move anything. It arms its own alarm next to whatever is already set.

Which means your phone's own alarms still ring, and OneAlarm can neither see them nor stop them. The remedy is to turn the Alarm toggle off inside your Sleep Schedule, and the app says so permanently rather than as a banner you can dismiss.

It cannot create one, delete one, change your bed temperature, or run the pump. Those addresses are refused before a request leaves the phone.

From the devices screen

Confirmed, saved, failed

The colour is not decoration. It is the difference between what was proved and what was merely accepted.

It tells you the truth about what happened.

Every remote write is read back. There are three outcomes, they are never blended, and they never share a colour or a word. This is a selling point rather than a disclaimer.

Confirmed

Written, read back, and the instant matched. Only a server that returns an absolute instant can earn this, which today means the bed.

Accepted, unconfirmed

The service took it, but it could not be read back, or it read back at a different time. Whoop returns no absolute instant, so its best available state is saved, permanently.

Failed

It did not happen, and the cause is named. The row also says what that device still holds, so a failure is a known time rather than an unknown one.

Colouring amber green would be claiming a confirmation the app does not have, and the moment that costs anything is 06:00.

Why the two are never blended

Before it sends

Nothing goes out before you have seen it.

There is a preview, and it shows two things: the resolved values, and the literal outbound request. It states plainly that nothing has been sent. An app that edits two live accounts on your behalf owes you the chance to look first.

Owner, or participant

Sole authorship is the aim, never the assumption. People use other apps, and that is information rather than an error.

Somebody else changed it.

She has an early flight, so she sets 05:00 in the Whoop app, the way anyone would. She does not open OneAlarm. The next time OneAlarm goes to write, it finds a time it did not put there.

An app that assumes it was the last one to write would flatten that silently, and this is not hypothetical. On the first night of real use a test rewrote a Monday to Friday Whoop alarm into every day, because the Whoop write replaces rather than merges. So the app compares before it writes, and when the two disagree it stops and says which alarm and which value.

Then it offers three, and adopting is often the right one. If she set 05:00 for a flight, the honest reading is that 05:00 is what she wants tomorrow, and the bed and the phone should follow the strap rather than fight it. Any device can be the one that gets changed.

  1. 1Follow the strap. The bed and the phone move to match what Whoop holds.
  2. 2Put the old time back. This overwrites what was set in the Whoop app, and says so.
  3. 3Leave the strap alone. The two disagree on purpose, and the screen keeps saying so.

There is no default button here on purpose. This one already cost a real schedule: a test rewrote a Monday to Friday alarm into every day, silently.

Printed at the bottom of that sheet

Local first

What stays on your phone.

  • Credentials and tokens live in the iOS Keychain, and nowhere else. Never in UserDefaults, never in plaintext, never logged, never committed.
  • There is no backend. Nothing about your night is stored on a server belonging to this app, because there is no such server.
  • No telemetry, and no third party analytics. Not reduced. Not anonymised. Absent.
  • Destructive, firmware and wipe commands are refused before a request leaves the phone. The allowlist is the boundary, and it sits on your side of the network.

Stated plainly, in the same type as everything else

Five of them

This is the section to read if you are deciding whether you want it. Everything above was easier to write than this.

What it cannot do.

  1. Your phone's own alarms still ring.

    OneAlarm cannot see or change a Clock alarm or the Health sleep schedule. Suppress a morning here and the Sleep Schedule alarm fires anyway. The remedy is to turn the Alarm toggle off inside your Sleep Schedule, and the app says so permanently, because you can create a Clock alarm at any time and nothing will notice.

  2. Whoop holds one schedule per account.

    So it cannot express two routines the way Eight Sleep can. The day list of whichever routine covers tonight is what gets written, and the other one is not represented on the strap.

  3. Whoop's server and your wrist are two claims.

    That the server holds the new time, and that the strap buzzes at the new time, are different statements. Only the first is verified. The second reaches the strap at its next sync with the Whoop app.

  4. There is no snooze.

    Apple's sleep alarm has a nine minute snooze. This has none. If you want nine more minutes you set the alarm nine minutes later, the night before, in the one place that moves all of it.

  5. It is not on the App Store, and it is not heading there.

    It uses Whoop's and Eight Sleep's own internal web services, which is a question about their terms rather than about the code. It is a personal, single account build. It uses no private Apple APIs, so a personal build and TestFlight internal testing are both open routes.
